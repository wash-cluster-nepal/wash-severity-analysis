ABOUT THE ANALYSIS

Nepal Flood WASH Severity Analysis

Why this analysis was developed

The late-August 2026 flood emergency caused widespread disruption across parts of central Nepal, damaging water-supply systems, affecting road access and displacing people into temporary holding centres. The analysis brings together available impact, service-disruption, current WASH-condition and vulnerability information so that users can quickly see where needs are greatest, what is driving severity, and which underlying indicators should be checked in more detail.

The analytical cohort covers 17 affected municipalities across six districts. Municipality-level affected-population estimates used in the model total 84,270 people. Holding-centre information is shown separately as site-level operational context and may include locations outside the 17-municipality analytical cohort.

What the final priority means

The final map is a prioritisation view across the 17 affected municipalities. It combines three groups of information: flood impact and WASH service disruption, current WASH conditions, and pre-existing vulnerability and aggravating factors. The class labels — Very High, High, Moderate, Low and Minimal — are intended to be read together with the underlying drivers rather than as stand-alone labels.

A municipality can therefore be Very High for different reasons. One municipality may have a very large affected population and major disruption to water-supply systems, while another may have lower direct impact but much higher pre-existing vulnerability. The municipality panel explicitly shows the dominant drivers.

The final class is based on fixed Need Score thresholds. Rank is shown separately for comparison across the 17 affected municipalities; there is no second rank-derived severity class in v15.

How the score is built

50%Flood & WSS impact

Uses the number and share of people affected and, where PDNA raw data are available, the scale and physical damage of assessed water-supply systems.

35%Current WASH conditions

Covers water access & service continuity, water quality & safety, and sanitation & hygiene. Current PDNA raw data support scoring of the first two; sanitation & hygiene is kept visible as a data gap.

15%Vulnerability & Aggravating Factors

Drinking-water vulnerability, sanitation vulnerability, poverty/inequality and physical access.

Overall Need Score = 50% P1 + 35% P2 + 15% P3. Where no current PDNA raw data are available, P2 is provisionally set to 2.5 and the lower evidence strength is shown.

Flood impact & WASH service disruption

This part of the model gives most weight to the scale of people affected and the scale of disruption to water-supply systems. Both absolute numbers and the share of the municipality affected are considered. The absolute magnitude is the starting point; a high proportional impact can increase the score, but a low proportional share cannot reduce a large absolute burden.

P2 is designed around three WASH areas: water access and service continuity, water quality and safety, and sanitation and hygiene. Current PDNA raw data provide comparable information for the first two. Sanitation and hygiene remains a data gap and will be added when comparable municipality-level information becomes available. Where no current PDNA raw data are available, a provisional score of 2.5 is used.

How to read the five-point scale

All scored indicators use the same direction: 5 = Very High, 4 = High, 3 = Moderate, 2 = Low, and 1 = Minimal. Higher values always mean greater severity, vulnerability or constraint. The interface emphasises the class label because it is easier to interpret; the numerical value remains visible as supporting detail.

Spatial and operational layers

The analytical choropleths can be viewed alongside the original flood-extent polygons, road-access constraints, ward boundaries and IOM holding-centre assessments. Flood extent is displayed as the actual polygon geometry. Road status is displayed as line geometry using four operational categories: Not Passable; Passable with restrictions/Damaged; Passable/Undamaged; and Others.

Data sources

The platform uses several datasets. Some are used directly in the severity score, while others are shown only as supporting or map context. The list below identifies each source and how it is used. Key scoring sources include affected-population estimates, PDNA raw water-supply-system assessment data, baseline vulnerability indicators and physical-access evidence. Supporting layers include IOM holding-centre assessments, government WSS damage information, flood extent, administrative boundaries and operational road status.

Important interpretation notes

People affected and population residing in affected wards are different measures and are shown separately. The latter should not be interpreted as the number of people affected. Municipality-level WASH-specific People in Need are not invented where no separate source exists.

Holding-centre information is site-level evidence and should not be treated as a complete municipality-wide assessment. The current WASH component remains provisional and should be updated as more systematic field evidence becomes available.
